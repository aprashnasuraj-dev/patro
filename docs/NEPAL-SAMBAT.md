# Nepal Sambat Suite: research, database and implementation guide

## 1. Why build this
- **Official status.** Since **11 Nov 2023**, Nepal Sambat (NS) appears in official government documents alongside Bikram Sambat. Lalitpur Metropolitan City has printed NS dates since NS 1140 (2020) and uses a *solar* NS for administration. The government decided in 2011 to adopt NS as a national calendar, and in 1999 declared Sankhadhar Sākhwā a national hero.
- **Nobody serves this community well.** The big patros show at most a single NS line. No one offers a full Newar festival calendar, the Newa script, the solar NS, or Mha Puja tools. Newars in the Valley, in trade towns across Nepal (Bandipur, Bhojpur, Tansen…) and in the diaspora are a loyal, culturally active audience.
- **You're already close.** Your API returns NS year, month, paksha and Newa-script month names, and I checked that they're correct (§6). This suite turns that one line into a full product.

## 2. How the calendar works (the rules implemented)

### 2.1 Lunar NS (traditional, used for every festival)
| Rule | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Epoch | NS 1 began 20 Oct 879 CE (reign of Rāghavadeva) | Wikipedia |
| Year | NS year N starts in autumn of CE **N + 879** | hyper-calendar PR, Wikipedia |
| New Year | **Kachhalā Thwa Pāru** = Kārtika shukla pratipadā = **Mha Puja** (4th day of Swanti) | Wikipedia (Mha Puja, Swanti) |
| Months | **Amānta** (new moon to new moon), Kachhalā first | hyper-calendar PR |
| Intercalary month | **अनला (Analā)** about every 3 years (a month with no sankranti) | Wikipedia |
| Dropped month | **न्हंला (Nhanlā)**, about every 2 decades | Wikipedia |
| Year length | 353–355 days, or 383–385 with Analā | Wikipedia |
| Reference | Kathmandu sunrise, Lahiri ayanamsa | hyper-calendar PR |

**The 12 months:**

| # | Devanagari | Newa | Roman | Amānta month | Full moon (पुन्हि) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | कछला | 𑐎𑐕𑐮𑐵 | Kachhalā | Kārtika | सकिमना पुन्हि |
| 2 | थिंला | 𑐠𑐶𑑄𑐮𑐵 | Thinlā | Mārgaśīrṣa | यःमरि पुन्हि |

\* Full-moon names marked * still need an expert to confirm them (see §7).

**Pakshas:** थ्वः (Thwa, bright) and गाः (Gā, dark).
**Tithi names:** पारु, द्वितीया, तृतीया, चौथी, पञ्चमी, **खस्ति**, सप्तमी … त्रयोदशी, then चतुर्दशी/**पुन्हि** in Thwa and **चह्रे**/**आमै** in Gā. These come from the Wikipedia month articles.

### 2.2 Solar NS (Lalitpur, administrative)
- New year is **20 October**. The month lengths are 30, 30, 30, 30, 30, **29 (Chaulā; 30 in a leap year)**, 31, 31, 31, 31, 31, 31.
- It's a leap year when (NS year + 880) is a Gregorian leap year.
- The mapping to Gregorian is fixed. For example, Christmas always falls on Pwanhelā 7 and 1 January on Pwanhelā 14.
- The source's worked example checks out: 1 June 2001 = NS 1121 Tachhalā 15.

### 2.3 Newa script (नेपाल लिपि / Prachalit)
- It's in Unicode block **U+11400–U+1147F** (added in Unicode 9.0, with additions through 13.0). I checked the code points against the Unicode database.
- It has special **murmured letters** that Devanagari writes as two characters: ङ्ह→𑐓, ञ्ह→𑐙, न्ह→𑐤, म्ह→𑐪, र्ह→𑐭, ल्ह→𑐯. The converter merges these into one letter each.
- Digits are 𑑐𑑑𑑒𑑓𑑔𑑕𑑖𑑗𑑘𑑙 (U+11450–9). The danda is 𑑋 (U+1144B) and the double danda 𑑌 (U+1144C).
- **Font:** Noto Sans Newa (Google Fonts, free OFL licence). Current browsers render it correctly. Older phones may show empty boxes, so always show Devanagari next to the Newa text.
- Converting Newa to and from Devanagari is **reliable for modern text** (names, dates, labels), but not for manuscripts.

## 3. The database
It's generated by `scripts/export-ns-db.ts` into `db/nepal-sambat/`.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `months.json` | The 12 months, the Analā/Nhanlā labels, and each month in Devanagari (with spelling variants), Newa, Roman and Sanskrit, plus its full-moon name, sources and status |
| `tithis.json` | Pakshas, 15 × 2 tithi names in 3 scripts, and weekdays |
| `festivals.json` | **46 festivals/jātrās.** Each has its date rule (lunar tithi + observance, or solar sankranti), length in days, tradition, places, a Nepali summary, public-holiday flag, a "declared annually" flag, sources and status |
| `festival-dates.json` | New Year, Analā months and every festival date for **NS 1140–1180** (1,886 rows) |
| `days.csv` | **14,972 days** (NS 1140–1180): AD → lunar NS (year, month, adhik, paksha, tithi) with labels in Devanagari and Newa, plus solar NS |
| `schema.sql` | Postgres/Supabase tables: `ns_months`, `ns_festivals`, `ns_festival_dates` (with editor confirmation), `ns_days`; public read access |
| `seed.sql` | Inserts for all of the above |
| `timeline.json` | The history timeline, 879 CE to 2023 |
| `sources.json` | Every source URL, referenced by key from each row |

**Festival coverage** (month by month): Mha Puja, Kijā Pujā, Saki Manā Punhi · Yomari Punhi · Jana Bāhā snān, Milā Punhi/Swasthāni · Shree Panchami, Si Punhi, Silā Charhe · Holi/Chir, Nālā Karunāmaya, **Pāhān Charhe**, Ghode Jātrā · Jana Bāhā Dyah Jātrā, Lhuti Punhi, Mātā Tīrtha · **Bunga Dyah Jātrā**, Swānyā Punhi · **Sithi Nakhah**, Jyā Punhi, Panauti Jātrā, Machā Tiyā · Harishayani, Dilā Punhi, **Gathān Mugah** · **Gunlā**, Nāg Panchami, Bahidyah Bwayegu, Kwānti Punhi, **Sā Pāru**, Matayā, Janmāshtami, Gokarna Aunsi · Chathā, **Yenyā**, Yenyā Punhi · Nahlā Swanegu, Khokana Rudrāyani, Kuchhi Bhwoy, Syākwah Tyākwah, Mohani Tikā, Kwah/Khichā/Sā Pujā · Ghyah Chāku Sanhu, **Biskā Jātrā** (solar).

**Admin workflow:** jātrās like the Rato Machhindranāth chariot days, Bhoto Jātrā and Biskā are set each year by guthis. Their dates are marked `declaredAnnually` and shown as "सम्भावित" (likely). An editor confirms the real date with `update ns_festival_dates set … confidence='confirmed'`, and the API then serves the confirmed date.

## 4. The code

```
src/patro-tools/nepal-sambat/
  data/calendar.ts      months, pakshas, tithis, solar rules, timeline, sources (source of truth)
  data/festivals.ts     46 festivals with rules, sources and status
  newa-script.ts        Devanagari ⇄ Newa, digits, font stack
  engine.ts             nsFromAd, adFromNs, nsNewYear, adhikMonthsInYear, formatNs (3 scripts),
                        solarNsFromAd / adFromSolarNs, festivalsOfYear, nextFestival
  react/NewaText.tsx          font + lang="new-Newa"
  react/useNsScript.tsx       remembered script choice + toggle
  react/NsTodayCard.tsx       today in 3 scripts, solar date, Mha Puja countdown, upcoming jātrās
  react/NsConverter.tsx       AD ⇄ lunar NS ⇄ solar NS (runs in the browser)
  react/NsMonthView.tsx       lunar month grid (server component)
  react/NewaLipiConverter.tsx Devanagari ⇄ Newa text tool
src/app-routes/api/v1/nepal-sambat/
  route.ts              GET ?ad=               today/any day, labels, solar, next new year, upcoming
  festivals/route.ts    GET ?year=1147         all festivals of an NS year
  convert/route.ts      GET ?ad= | ?ns=1146-11-ga-3[&adhik=1] | ?solar=1147-3-7
  ics/route.ts          GET ?year=             Google/Apple Calendar feed
src/app-pages/
  nepal-sambat/page.tsx             hub (server-rendered, no loading placeholders)
  nepal-sambat/[year]/page.tsx      one page per year (static)
  nepal-sambat/jatra/[id]/page.tsx  one page per festival with Event JSON-LD
  newa-lipi/page.tsx                script converter
scripts/export-ns-db.ts             builds db/nepal-sambat/*
tests/nepal-sambat.test.ts          21 tests
```

**Speed:** `nsFromAd` takes about 1 ms. Working out a full year's festivals takes about 0.7 s, so call `festivalsOfYear` at build time, from ISR or cron jobs, or read from `ns_festival_dates`. Never compute a whole year per request without caching.

## 5. Integrating it into your app
1. Copy `src/patro-tools/nepal-sambat` (it depends on `core/` and `tithi-events/` from the tools kit, which you already have).
2. Run `supabase db push` with `schema.sql`, then `psql -f seed.sql`, then `\copy ns_days from 'days.csv' csv header`.
3. **Use your own engine as the primary source.** Your `/api/v1/today` already computes NS. Keep it, and run this engine in CI as a cross-check: for every day in `days.csv`, compare the year, month, adhik flag, paksha and tithi.
4. Add `Noto Sans Newa` in the root layout (`next/font/google`).
5. Add the pages: `/nepal-sambat`, `/nepal-sambat/[year]`, `/nepal-sambat/jatra/[id]` and `/newa-lipi`. Add a card on `/tools` and an **NS line with a script toggle** on the home date card.
6. Growth features:
   - Mha Puja / "न्हूदँया भिन्तुना" greeting cards in Newa script, from your `/card` tool.
   - A "ns" command in the Viber/Telegram bot.
   - A Mha Puja countdown for the diaspora in their local time.
   - A festival ICS subscription.
   - The Newa-script converter as its own page that search engines can find.

**Two things to check in your current API:**
1. **Tithi names.** Your API returns names like `"Pāmilā / Pāru"` and `"Swamilā"` (for Tritiyā). The sources I found use पारु, द्वितीया, तृतीया … खस्ति … चह्रे/पुन्हि/आमै. If "‑milā" names are a real usage, cite a source on `/sources`. If not, switch to the forms above.
2. **The intercalary month label.** Your API shows Analā as a standalone month `n: 13` with no link to the month it repeats. Users (and priests) need to know *which* month is doubled, so show **"अनला (तछला)"**. No single written form is established, so showing both is the safest choice.

## 7. Needs expert review before launch
Mark these as reviewed in the data (`status: 'review'` → `'sourced'`) after checking with a **Nepal Bhasa scholar or the Nepal Bhasa Academy** and a Newar jyotishi:
- The full-moon names for Chilā, Gunlā and Kaulā
- **Nepal Bhasa weekday names** (आइतबाः … शनिबाः): common usage, but no cited source yet
- Newar names for the Mohani days (Nahlā Swanegu, Kuchhi Bhwoy, Syākwah Tyākwah) and the Mātā Tīrtha / Gokarna Aunsi terms
- Kwānti Punhi as the name for Gunlā Punhi
- Mataya's tithi (Gā 2); Panauti Jātrā's tithi; the Jana Bāhā snān date
- **Biskā Jātrā:** it's solar (around Mesh Sankranti), set here as sankranti −4 days, 9 days long. Confirm the local rule.
- Which days are public holidays (`publicHoliday: 'check'`). Sync these with your MoHA holiday table.
- The spelling standard for months: the Wikipedia forms (प्वँहेला, सिला, चिला, दिला) or the doubled-consonant forms (पोहेला, सिल्ला, चिल्ला, दिल्ला). Both are stored, so just pick which one to display.

An **advisory credit on `/sources`** (for example "reviewed by …") builds the cultural credibility this suite is meant to earn.
